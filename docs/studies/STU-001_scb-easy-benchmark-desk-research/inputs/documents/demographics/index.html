    What belongs on this page

    Who exists in the market, in countable terms. Population-level structure: age, income, region, device, connectivity. This page describes the population, not SCB’s users specifically — that is [[who-uses-scb-easy]].

    SCB’s target segments — who the bank is aiming at

    The population is not the target market, and this page was previously written as though it were. SCB is a commercial bank with a stated tilt toward affluent and rising-affluent customers, and its product decisions follow from that.

    Segments reported as SCB’s own framing:

    SegmentAs described
    Young professionals and millennialsEarly-career customers needing basic lending, savings and digital tools
    Emerging wealthYounger rising earners building personal wealth
    Career growth and business buildersMid-stage professionals expanding businesses, via hybrid digital and human advisory
    Financially secure clientsHigh-net-worth customers, long-term stability and intergenerational wealth transfer

    Status: unverified. These segment names circulate in secondary summaries attributed to scb.co.th and trade press. They have not been read at an SCB source, and segment naming is exactly the kind of thing that gets paraphrased into something tidier than the original. Confirm before using in a deliverable.

    The product set corroborates the tilt, which is the stronger evidence. WEALTH4U investment advisory, EASY-D debenture accounts and the SCB Gold Marketplace ([[what-scb-easy-offers]]) are not features built for a mass-market transactional user. Nor is “hybrid digital and human advisory”, which implies a customer worth a human’s time.

    A caution on the 17 million / 80% figure

    Secondary summaries pair these segments with “roughly 17 million clients, around 80% — 14 million — actively using SCB Easy”.

    That is not new evidence. It traces to the same early-2023 press interview already logged in [[scb-easy-scale-public-record]] as low-medium confidence, and the “14 million” is produced by multiplying a rounded 17 million by a rounded 80%. Two approximations multiplied together, presented as a precise number. It also conflicts with the range of other published figures, 11m to 18m.

    Treat it as the same unverified claim wearing a more confident face.

    The tension this creates

    SCB targets the affluent. SCB Easy serves everyone SCB banks. Those are different populations, and since Easy Net was retired the app is the only self-service channel for both ([[who-uses-scb-easy]]).

    So the app carries two jobs that pull apart:

    • For the target segments — wealth products, advisory, investment, density, capability.
    • For everyone else, including the segment below — transfers and bills, operated sometimes by a family member on a device the account holder does not own.

    Neither group is optional. The bank’s growth case sits with the first; its regulatory, reputational and inclusion exposure sits with the second. A single home screen has to serve both, and that is a segmentation problem before it is a design problem.

    It also reframes the exclusion evidence below. The elderly and digitally excluded are not SCB’s target market — and that is precisely why the risk is real. Populations a bank is not designing for are the ones most easily designed out, and here being designed out means losing access to self-service banking entirely.

    Age and financial exclusion

    Where the population data below matters most: it describes the group SCB is not aiming at but is still obliged to serve.

    The clearest and best-sourced pattern available.

    ClaimFigureSource
    Unbanked skewMore than 50% of Thais without an account are over 55ScienceDirect
    Elderly internet use~21.2% of elderly Thais use the internetScienceDirect
    Device ownership44.6% personally owned a device (vulnerable older adults, Lampang)PIER
    Income in that sample61% earned under THB 3,000/monthPIER
    Proxy operationMany rely on children or grandchildren to complete digital registrationPIER
    Fraud exposureFraud disproportionately affects older adults in ThailandGlobal Findex 2025, via World Bank

    Note the sample. The PIER figures come from vulnerable older adults in Lampang, one of Thailand’s most aged provinces. That is a deliberately hard case, not a national average. Treat it as the lower bound of digital capability, not the middle.

    Compound problem, not two separate ones. Proxy operation by a family member, plus elevated fraud exposure, plus an app that is now the only self-service channel ([[who-uses-scb-easy]]). Generic accessibility compliance does not address someone else holding the phone. Feeds H5 in [[STU-001]].

    Urban and upcountry

    Thin. One dated figure: mobile phone users at 91.5% urban against 87.9% rural by 2018 (NSO, via secondary reporting). Eight years old and pre-dates the PromptPay era.

    This is a real gap. The Bangkok-to-upcountry split is a standing assumption in Thai product work and we currently cannot evidence it. ETDA’s annual Thailand Internet User Behaviour survey is the obvious source and has not yet been opened.

    Device and platform

    Nothing yet. iOS and Android share for Thailand is not established here. It sits in the cultural analysis stream as a behaviour question, but the raw split belongs on this page.

    What we do not know

    • National age distribution of mobile banking users, as opposed to the elderly edge case
    • Income distribution of the banked population
    • Regional breakdown of app usage
    • Platform split, device age, OS version spread
    • Connectivity quality outside urban centres

    Evidence ceiling

    Population statistics describe a market. They never describe a person. Nothing here grounds a persona or a need; it establishes the conditions a design has to work within.

    The specific trap on this page is treating “over 55” as a segment. Age is a demographic fact, not a behaviour. Two 70-year-olds — one a retired accountant with an iPhone, one dependent on a grandchild — are not one group. That distinction belongs in [[archetypes]].
