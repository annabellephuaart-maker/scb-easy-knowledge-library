    Reading the same moment across every app at once.

    Where the per-app pages go deep on one product, this page reads across them. The unit of comparison is the screen archetype — the home screen, the transfer confirmation, the login — so that the same moment can be set side by side across nine or twenty apps.

    That is the comparison that produces findings. One app’s home screen tells you what one team decided; nine home screens in a row tell you what the category assumes, and where somebody broke from it.

    The colleague’s artefact

    A comparison tool already exists, built in-house: the SCBX Screen Estate. It holds 145 screens across the nine SCBX consumer apps, filed by archetype and grouped by business pillar, with a compare view that lays one archetype across all nine at once.

    To be integrated here. It is currently a standalone HTML artefact held outside the repo. Its coverage, limits and the three findings drawn from it are recorded in the retired inventory page, which this page supersedes as the live home for cross-app comparison.

    Carry forward, because it constrains what this page can claim:

    • 110 of its 145 screens are storefront creatives, not interface. Only 35 are real in-app captures, covering two apps of nine.
    • Login and onboarding has 2 screens; cross-app handoff has 1. The thinnest archetypes are the ones that matter most here.
    • It is a dated snapshot. Apps ship continuously.

    Two archetypes are missing from this list and need adding as capture extends beyond the SCBX portfolio: AI surfaces and error and empty states. Both are central to this project and neither has a home in the current scheme.

    What to compare, once screens exist

    1. Home screen — what each team believes the user came to do. The clearest single expression of a product’s model of its user.
    2. Transfer or pay flow — the tier 1 journey, against a two-second QR benchmark.
    3. Login and onboarding — the highest-friction moment, and the thinnest evidence.
    4. Confirmation — how certainty is communicated once money has moved.
    5. AI surfaces — disclosure, consent, opt-out, fallback.
    6. Error and empty states — where quality actually shows.

    Evidence ceiling

    A screen comparison shows what teams decided. It cannot show whether any of it worked. Nothing here grounds a user need, and a pattern appearing in every app may be an industry habit rather than a solved problem.
