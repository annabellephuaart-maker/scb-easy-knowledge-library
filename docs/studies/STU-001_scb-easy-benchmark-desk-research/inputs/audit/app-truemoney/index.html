    Thailand: TrueMoney Wallet

    Status

    Type

    Evidence quality

    Confidence

    Screens

    The largest non-bank money app in Thailand. A competitor SCB Easy may not think of as one.

    1. About the company

    Operated by Ascend Money, part of the Charoen Pokphand (CP) group — the conglomerate that also owns 7-Eleven in Thailand. Ascend Money holds an e-money licence rather than a banking licence.

    The ownership is the whole story. CP owns the country’s dominant convenience-store network, and TrueMoney is accepted across it. A bank has branches; TrueMoney has 7-Eleven.

    It also operates roughly 39,000 agent outlets, reaching cash-centric provinces where a bank app’s assumptions about smartphones and bank accounts break down. Source: Fintech News SG.

    2. About the product

    A stored-value e-wallet: top up with cash or transfer, then spend at 7-Eleven, online, on transport and bills. Top-up is the important half — it is the on-ramp from cash into digital for people who do not start with a bank balance.

    MeasureFigure
    Share of Thai e-wallet market~53% (Antom puts it at 52.6%)
    Active users17m+
    Agent outlets~39,000
    TrueMoney + LINE Pay share of e-money floatover 75%

    Source: Fintech News SG.

    It has also extended beyond QR into tap-to-pay with BlueTap, adding EMV-style contactless from the wallet — a move onto card rails from a non-bank. Source: GlobeNewswire, GlobalData.

    3. About the app

    Store metrics not yet retrieved. To collect on the same basis as the rest of this stream: iOS and Play ratings, install band, version cadence, minimum OS.

    Sector context that bounds what those numbers will mean: e-money across Thailand runs about 493 million transactions a month against PromptPay’s 2.26 billion — roughly a fifth, despite 112 million e-money accounts. Wallets are widely held and comparatively lightly used. See [[contactless-payment-behaviour-th]].

    4. Key screens

    Not yet captured.

    Priority, and different from the bank apps: cash top-up at an agent or 7-Eleven, the pay-in- store moment, stored-value balance display, and onboarding without a bank account — the journey no bank app in this set has to solve.

    5. Design breakdown

    Not yet assessable — requires captures.

    Illustration · Assets · Design atmosphere · Colour · App UI

    The question for this page is not “how do we compare” but “what does an app look like when it was designed for people the bank app was not designed for?”

    TrueMoney serves lower-income, cash-preferring, upcountry and unbanked users at scale — the same population [[demographics]] identifies as digitally excluded from banking. Whatever it does about literacy, language, cash handling and trust is a working answer to a problem SCB has not solved.

    Two things to look for specifically: how it explains stored value to someone who thinks in cash, and whether its visual register is more approachable than a bank’s, or simply louder.

    Why this belongs in the competitive set

    TrueMoney is not a bank and does not want to be. But it holds money, moves money, and is accepted in more physical locations than SCB has branches. For a segment of Thai users it is the primary money app, and SCB Easy is the account they top up from.

    That makes it a competitor for the everyday payment moment even though it competes for nothing else — and the everyday payment moment is 75% of what SCB Easy does ([[the-core-use-case]]).

    Open questions

    • OQ: What are TrueMoney’s store metrics on the same basis as the bank apps?
    • OQ: What proportion of TrueMoney top-ups come from bank transfers, and from which banks?
    • OQ: Do users hold both, and how do they choose at the counter?
    • OQ: How does onboarding work for someone without a bank account?
