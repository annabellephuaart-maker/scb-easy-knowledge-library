    Who the alternatives serve, and where they overlap.

    What belongs on this page

    Audiences, not interfaces. Who each competitor reaches, how many, and how engaged they are. How a competitor’s screens are built belongs in the competitive analysis stream; who uses them belongs here.

    The reason to keep them apart: a pattern that works for a competitor’s audience may not work for yours, and you can only judge that if you know whose audience it is.

    K PLUS — the domestic benchmark

    The one competitor figure that is properly sourced.

    MeasureFigure
    Users24.2m at end-2025
    Growth+5% year-on-year
    Active rate78% monthly-active, defined as logging in at least once per month

    Source: Asian Banker, reporting KASIKORNBANK.

    Two things make this the strongest number in the study. It is dated, and it carries its own definition of active. That is what a citable figure looks like.

    The comparison cannot be completed. SCB Easy’s equivalent is unestablished — see [[scb-easy-scale-public-record]]. The ratio is somewhere between 1.3x and 2.2x. The “twice our size” framing has been withdrawn from the study until the denominator is real.

    Watch the asymmetry itself. KBank publishes a defined, dated, engagement-qualified figure. SCB publishes undated marketing copy. That is a difference in how the two organisations treat measurement, and it may show up in how each team builds.

    Other domestic players

    An older, period-unclear source put Thailand at 33m mobile banking users with K PLUS at 48% share. Do not use it: the period is unstated and it conflicts with the 24.2m figure above.

    No published user-base figures were found for Krungthai NEXT, Bangkok Bank or ttb touch.

    Like-for-like on the stores

    K PLUS and Krungthai NEXT sit a full install band above SCB Easy. This is the closest thing to a like-for-like scale comparison the public record offers: same store, same metric, same market. It supports H1 in [[STU-001]] more directly than any user-base figure found so far — though a band is crude, 10M+ spans 10 to 50 million, and it does not close the question in [[scb-easy-scale-public-record]].

    Read Krungthai NEXT’s band with care. Krungthai is the state bank and runs Thailand’s government welfare and stimulus schemes. Installs driven by a benefit payment are exactly the mandated-install case flagged in the rating caveat on the overview — a download that was not a choice. Treat its 50M+ as distribution, not preference.

    The store divergence is systematic, not an SCB problem. Every app here rates 4.3–4.7 on Play and 3.40–3.92 on iOS. That pattern across five independent apps means the gap is a property of the stores and their populations, not evidence that any one iOS app is worse.

    What survives the correction: SCB Easy is at or near the bottom in both stores — joint lowest on Play at 4.3, lowest on iOS at 3.40. Consistent across two populations is a stronger signal than lowest in one.

    Two things this changes

    The store matters more than expected. SCB Easy rates 4.3 on Google Play and 3.40 on the iOS App Store. That is not a small gap. It means the portfolio comparison in [[scbx-screen-estate]], which used Play ratings, cannot be set beside these numbers — comparing a Play rating to an App Store rating compares two different populations under two different rating prompts. Compare within a store, never across.

    The Play figures answer the question this originally raised. Thai iOS users rate more harshly across the board; this is not an SCB-specific iOS defect.

    The whole category rates poorly. Nothing here clears 4.0. Thai retail banking apps are, by their users’ account, mediocre across the board. That reframes the opportunity: the bar to beat is low, and no competitor has found the answer either.

    Caveats that still apply: ratings are lifetime cumulative, so old problems keep voting; they can be brigaded; and they measure sentiment, not usability.

    Regional and global comparators

    No audience data gathered. The set proposed in the competitive analysis stream is DBS digibank, Kakao Bank, Nubank and Revolut.

    Note a limit before spending time here: these serve different markets under different regulation with different payment infrastructure. Their audience numbers are context, never a target. Nubank’s scale says nothing about what is achievable in Thailand.

    Adjacent, non-banking

    Untouched, and arguably more important than the regional set. Grab and similar high-use Thai apps set the interaction expectations users carry into a banking app, whether or not the bank competes with them. Audience overlap with SCB Easy is likely high and entirely unmeasured here.

    Evidence ceiling

    Competitor audience size tells you the scale of an alternative. It does not tell you why anyone chose it, or what they would switch for. A user who has both K PLUS and SCB Easy installed appears in both counts and tells you nothing about preference.
