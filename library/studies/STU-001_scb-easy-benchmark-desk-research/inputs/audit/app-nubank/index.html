    The case that matters most to SCB, for a reason that is easy to miss.

    1. About the company

    Nubank, founded in Brazil, now one of the largest digital banks in the world by customer count, with operations in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia.

    Scale, customer numbers and financial performance are widely reported but not yet verified at source here.

    2. About the product

    Began with a no-fee credit card, expanded into accounts, lending and investing. Built branchless from the start.

    Why Nubank is the most relevant global reference for this project — and it is not the design. Nubank grew by serving people the incumbent banks had effectively excluded: those for whom branch banking was inaccessible, expensive, or humiliating. Financial inclusion was the business model, not a corporate responsibility line.

    [[demographics]] describes an excluded population in Thailand: over half of the unbanked are over 55, only around a fifth of elderly Thais use the internet, and many operate digital services through a family member. SCB Easy is now the only self-service channel ([[who-uses-scb-easy]]), which means exclusion from the app is exclusion from the bank.

    Nubank is the standing example of a product that treated that population as the market rather than as an edge case. Whether its answers transfer is a separate question.

    Illustration · Assets · Design atmosphere · Colour · App UI

    Two questions specific to this page.

    How does it explain banking to someone who has never had it? Language, disclosure, defaults, and what it assumes the user already understands. That is the transferable skill for a Thai bank facing an excluded segment, and it is mostly a content-design problem rather than a visual one.

    How does a strongly branded product stay legible? Nubank is known for a single saturated brand colour used confidently. Worth examining how that survives contact with accessibility contrast requirements and with dense financial data.

    The limit on this comparison

    Brazil’s banking market, regulation and payment infrastructure differ from Thailand’s, and Brazil has no PromptPay equivalent in the way Thailand does — Pix arrived later and into a different competitive position. Nubank’s scale says nothing about what is achievable here.

    Borrow the posture toward excluded users. Do not borrow the growth story.

    Open questions

    • OQ: What does Nubank do specifically for low-literacy or first-time banking users?
    • OQ: How does its brand colour perform against WCAG AA in-product?
    • OQ: How did Pix change Nubank’s product, and does that parallel PromptPay for SCB?
